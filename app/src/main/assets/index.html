📚 EVIDENCE-INFORMED GUIDE

Learning About Keto

Safety, metabolism, testing, nutrients & clinical guidance (Evidence through Oct 2026)

⚠️ Important Medical Notice

Educational information only — not a diagnosis or treatment plan. Keto can require rapid medication changes, especially for diabetes and blood-pressure treatment. Children, pregnant or breastfeeding people, and anyone with diabetes, kidney, liver, pancreatic or metabolic disease should obtain professional guidance before starting.

1

What is Keto?

A ketogenic diet is a very-low-carbohydrate, high-fat eating pattern designed to produce nutritional ketosis — a regulated state in which the liver synthesizes ketone bodies from fat, providing an alternative cellular fuel source for the brain, heart, and muscle.

The Three Ketone Bodies:
🩸 Beta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB)

Primary circulating ketone in blood. Measured via finger-prick blood meters (0.5 – 3.0 mmol/L).

🧪 Acetoacetate (AcAc)

Filtered into urine. Measured via urine dipsticks (most informative in first 2-4 weeks).

🌬️ Acetone

Volatile compound expelled through the breath. Detected by handheld breath analyzers.

🟢 Nutritional Ketosis

Regulated physiological state. Insulin remains present. Blood pH is normal. Ketones 0.5–3.0 mmol/L.

🔴 Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)

Life-threatening medical emergency. Severe insulin absence, uncontrolled ketone surge (>10 mmol/L), severe metabolic acidosis.

2

Types of Ketogenic Diet

Percentages indicate percentage of total energy (% kcal), not food weight:

Classic Ketogenic (4:1 Ratio) Clinical / Epilepsy Therapy
90% Fat
6% P
4% C

4 grams of fat for every 1 gram of protein + carbohydrate combined. Strict medical supervision.

Standard Consumer Keto Most Popular
70-80% Fat
15-25% P
5% C

Daily net carbs capped at ≤ 20-30g. Moderate protein to preserve lean tissue, high fat for satiety.

Modified Atkins Diet (MAD) Flexible Medical Protocol
65% Fat
29-32% P
3-6% C

Carbs restricted to 10-20g/day without restricting protein or overall calories.

MCT Oil Ketogenic Diet High Ketone Efficiency
70% Fat (MCT)
19% P
10% C

Medium-chain triglycerides (MCTs) yield more ketones per calorie, allowing slightly more protein and vegetables.

Low Glycemic Index Treatment (LGIT) Liberal Carbohydrate
60% Fat
20-30% P
15% C

Permits 40-60g carbs/day, strictly selecting carbohydrates with Glycemic Index under 50.

Keto and Women

🌸 PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome)

Clinical trials show significant improvements in insulin sensitivity, hyperandrogenism, LH/FSH ratios, and body weight. However, long-term fertility outcomes remain an active area of investigation.

🩸 Menstrual Cycle Changes

While insulin-resistant women often see more regular cycles, sudden severe calorie restriction or low energy availability can trigger hypothalamic amenorrhoea. Seek medical care if cycles stop for 3+ months.

🦴 Menopause & Bone Health

Evidence suggests positive metabolic support in postmenopausal women with obesity. However, bone mineral density protection requires prioritizing calcium, vitamin D, adequate protein, and resistance training.

🚫 Pregnancy & Breastfeeding Warning

Do not begin unsupervised weight-loss keto while pregnant or nursing. Nutrient demands rise significantly, and rare cases of lactation-ketoacidosis have been documented.

4

Potential Health Benefits

🧠 Drug-Resistant Epilepsy

The most historically validated clinical application; significantly reduces seizure frequency under specialist care.

⚖️ Short-Term Weight Loss & Appetite Control

Suppresses ghrelin (hunger hormone) and increases peptide YY (satiety), reducing spontaneous caloric intake.

📉 Triglycerides & HDL Cholesterol

One of the most robust metabolic outcomes: blood triglycerides drop substantially while HDL typically rises.

🩸 Type 2 Diabetes Glycemic Control

Can dramatically lower HbA1c and fasting blood glucose, frequently reducing the need for glucose-lowering drugs.

5

Who Should Avoid Keto?

Do Not Start Unsupervised:
  • Type 1 Diabetes: High risk of severe ketoacidosis without precise insulin adjustments.
  • SGLT2-Inhibitor Users: Drugs like dapagliflozin, empagliflozin carry risk of euglycaemic DKA.
  • Pancreatic, Gallbladder, or Severe Liver/Kidney Disease: Inability to properly digest or process fats.
  • Familial Hypercholesterolaemia (FH): Baseline LDL is already genetically elevated (LDLR mutations). High saturated-fat keto can trigger drastic LDL-C and ApoB surges. However, with close physician oversight, lipid-lowering therapies (e.g. statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors), and prioritizing monounsaturated fats (olive oil, avocados, fish) over heavy animal saturated fats, cardiovascular risk can be monitored and managed safely.
  • Active Eating Disorders or Severe Malnutrition.
🧬 Absolute Metabolic Contraindications:

Primary carnitine deficiency, CPT-I / CPT-II deficiency, Carnitine translocase deficiency, Fatty-acid beta-oxidation defects, Pyruvate carboxylase deficiency, Porphyria, and Glycogen-storage disease type I.

6

Advanced Biology & Chemistry

The molecular mechanisms that power fat breakdown and ketone synthesis:

1. Lipolysis (Fat Release)
Triacylglycerol + 3 H₂O ➔ ATGL / HSL ➔ Glycerol + 3 Free Fatty Acids

Carb reduction lowers insulin and elevates glucagon, activating Adipose Triglyceride Lipase (ATGL) and Hormone-Sensitive Lipase (HSL).

2. The Carnitine Shuttle
Fatty acyl-CoA ➔ CPT-I ➔ Acylcarnitine ➔ Translocase ➔ CPT-II ➔ Matrix acyl-CoA

Long-chain fatty acids cannot cross the inner mitochondrial membrane alone. Carnitine palmitoyltransferase (CPT-I & CPT-II) shuttles them inside.

3. Hepatic Ketogenesis Pathway
2 Acetyl-CoA ➔ Thiolase ➔ Acetoacetyl-CoA ➔ HMGCS2 ➔ HMG-CoA ➔ HMG-CoA lyase ➔ Acetoacetate ➔ BHB & Acetone

Why the liver doesn't use ketones: The liver lacks the enzyme SCOT (succinyl-CoA:3-ketoacid CoA transferase), meaning it manufactures ketones purely to export them to the brain and body!

Nutrient Risk Cheat Sheet

Cutting processed grains and starchy root vegetables can displace critical micronutrients if your meals lack variety:

🧂 Sodium
Why risk rises: Early renal sodium/water dumping due to lower insulin.
Food sources: Bone broth, olives, pickles, feta, salted food.
🥑 Potassium
Why risk rises: Reduced fruit and starchy tuber intake.
Food sources: Avocado, spinach, mushrooms, salmon, Greek yogurt.
🌰 Magnesium
Why risk rises: Whole grains and legumes restricted.
Food sources: Pumpkin seeds, almonds, dark leafy greens, cacao.
🧀 Calcium
Why risk rises: When dairy is limited or displaced.
Food sources: Hard cheeses, Greek yogurt, canned sardines with bones.
🐟 Vitamin D
Why risk rises: Very few rich natural dietary sources.
Food sources: Mackerel, wild salmon, egg yolks, sunlight.
🥬 Folate (B9)
Why risk rises: Enriched grain flour and beans removed.
Food sources: Spinach, asparagus, avocado, broccoli.
🥩 Thiamine (B1)
Why risk rises: Fortified cereal and bread eliminated.
Food sources: Pork, trout, sunflower seeds, pecans, walnuts.
🥦 Vitamin C
Why risk rises: High-sugar fruits omitted.
Food sources: Bell peppers, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, raspberries.

Antioxidants, Herbs & Spices

Keto is naturally rich in anti-inflammatory phytochemicals when incorporating vibrant herbs and spices:

🌿 Rosemary & Thyme

Rich in carnosic acid and thymol. High antioxidant capacity in culinary oils and meats.

🌱 Oregano

Abundant in rosmarinic acid and carvacrol; pairs excellently with eggs and tomato dishes.

🌰 Cinnamon & Ginger

Contains cinnamaldehyde & gingerols. Great in keto teas, baking, and dressings.

🍫 Unsweetened 100% Cacao

Highest food source of flavanols; supports endothelial vascular function.

✨ The Turmeric + Black Pepper Synergy

Curcumin in turmeric has naturally low oral bioavailability. Combining it with black pepper (piperine) enhances intestinal absorption by up to 2,000% by inhibiting hepatic and intestinal glucuronidation.

Carbs versus Net Carbs (Label Guide)

Understanding international nutrition facts label conventions prevents common calculation mistakes:

🇪🇺 European & Romanian Labels
Net Carbs = Declared Carbohydrates

In the EU and UK, dietary fibre is legally classified separately from total carbohydrates. Do NOT subtract fibre again! (Subtracting fibre from an EU label results in false/impossible negative numbers).

🇺🇸 US Nutrition Facts Labels
Net Carbs = Total Carbs − Fibre − Polyols

In the US, total carbohydrate includes dietary fibre and sugar alcohols. You must explicitly subtract dietary fibre and approved polyols (like erythritol).

🍬 Sugar Alcohols (Polyols): Erythritol has a glycemic index of 0 and is subtracted 100%. Maltitol has a meaningful glycemic impact (GI ~35) and should only be subtracted partially.

Testing Ketosis

Blood Beta-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) is the clinical gold standard for measuring nutritional ketosis:

Blood BHB Spectrum (mmol/L) Target Zone: 0.5 – 3.0
< 0.5 (Low) 0.5 – 1.0 (Mild) 1.0 – 3.0 (Optimal) > 3.0 (Fasting/Caution)
🩸 Blood Ketone Meter

Measures active BHB directly. Most accurate, real-time quantitative reading.

🧪 Urine Dipsticks

Measures excess acetoacetate. Inexpensive in early weeks, but fades as kidneys adapt.

🌬️ Breath Acetone Analyzer

Reusable and painless. Good for monitoring trends without purchasing disposable test strips.

Fasting & Protein Myths

⏳ Does Fasting Help Ketosis?

Fasting rapidly accelerates ketosis because liver glycogen stores empty without replacement. However, fasting is not mandatory for ketosis. Prioritize hydration and electrolytes if fasting, and avoid prolonged fasts without clinical oversight.

🥩 Can Protein Stop Ketosis? (The Myth)

The idea that "protein turns into chocolate cake via gluconeogenesis" is a myth. Gluconeogenesis is a demand-driven physiological process, not supply-driven. Prioritizing protein protects lean muscle mass, boosts TEF (thermic effect of food), and enhances satiety.

13

Alcohol & Keto Beverages

The liver prioritizes metabolizing ethanol over fat and ketones:

Ethanol ➔ Alcohol Dehydrogenase ➔ Acetaldehyde ➔ Aldehyde Dehydrogenase ➔ Acetate
💡 Important: Ketosis vs. Glycemia Clarification

Zero-carb spirits (whiskey, vodka, gin) do NOT spike blood glucose or insulin: They will not kick you out of ketosis via carbohydrates. However, because alcohol is a metabolic toxin, the liver halts fatty-acid oxidation (fat burning) until ethanol is completely eliminated.

Why Hypoglycemia (NOT high sugar) is the real risk: Ethanol metabolism blocks hepatic gluconeogenesis (the liver's ability to produce new glucose). On a keto diet or during fasting, liver glycogen stores are already low. Drinking alcohol prevents the liver from releasing glucose into the blood, which can trigger acute hypoglycemia (dangerously low blood sugar). Never drink on an empty stomach when keto-adapted.

Keto Beverage Guide:
🟢 Excellent (Daily)

Water (still/sparkling), unsweetened green/black tea, black coffee, bone broth.

🟡 Compatible

Coffee with heavy cream, sugar-free electrolyte water, unsweetened almond milk.

🔴 Unsuitable

Cow's milk (lactose), fruit juices, regular beer, sugary cocktails, energy drinks.

🧬 Fasting & Ketosis Biology Stages
12 Hours: Glycogen Depletion

Liver glycogen levels drop significantly. Blood glucose drops and insulin levels decrease, preparing the liver to burn body fat.

14–16 Hours: Accelerated Ketone Production

Fatty acids are oxidized into Acetoacetate & Beta-Hydroxybutyrate (BHB). Ketone levels rise, providing stable mental focus.

18–24 Hours: Cellular Autophagy

Cellular recycling activates (clearing damaged proteins and senescent cell components) via AMPK activation and mTOR downregulation.

24+ Hours: Deep Reset & Maximum Insulin Sensitivity

Maximum insulin reset and peak human growth hormone (HGH) secretion. Maintain adequate salt and electrolyte intake.
